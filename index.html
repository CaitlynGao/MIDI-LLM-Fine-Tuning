Empowering MuseStudio with MIDI-LLM Fine-Tuning

Why MIDI-LLM?

The Combination of MIDI and large language models is ideal for AI-driven music structuring because it combines the symbolic precision of MIDI with the reasoning power of large language models, allowing the system to understand music not just as isolated notes or velocities, but as a coherent, expressive language. By representing musical events as text-like tokens tied to musical semantics—rhythm, harmony, phrasing, and dynamics—MIDI-LLM can generate structurally consistent compositions, suggest contextually appropriate progressions, and adapt stylistically to user intent. Unlike waveform-based audio models, it maintains musical intelligibility, which accelerates the workflow for producers and supports creative exploration without sacrificing musical coherence.

Most Recent Work: Fine-Tuning to Enhance Musical Interpretation

While LLMs are powerful, they aren’t inherently designed for music. In many AI music applications, the model functions like a manager—it can organize notes and follow rules, but it doesn’t truly “feel” or understand the flow, emotion, and structure of a piece. To address this limitation, I developed a specialized fine-tuning process tailored for musical reasoning. Rather than having the model process raw numerical data, I created a “textualized” musical language that translates complex MIDI information into meaningful symbols. These symbols capture rhythm, harmony, phrasing, and dynamics, allowing the model to “read” music as a language rather than a sequence of numbers. With this approach, the model can reason about musical ideas, anticipate structural patterns, and make suggestions that feel musically intuitive—essentially giving it a native-like understanding of music.

The Music Symbolic System

To help the AI understand structure and rhythm, I designed a streamlined notation system. Each note is represented by a concise string of data:

[Start Time][Pitch][Duration]@[Velocity]

A. Start Time

Represented by a 2-digit number (e.g., 00, 08, 15). This indicates which 16th note the sound begins on within a specific measure.

B. Pitch Mapping

We use a standard "Note Name + Octave" format for clarity.

SymbolCcDdEFfGgAaB
NoteCC#DD#EFF#GG#AA#B

Example: C3, f4 (F#4), A5

C. Rhythm (Duration)

A 2-digit number representing the length of the note, measured in 16th-note units.

D. Velocity (Dynamics)

To simplify MIDI's 0-127 range for the AI, we categorize intensity into 6 levels:

LevelMIDI RangeDescription
@11 - 20Softest
@221 - 40Soft
@341 - 60Medium
@461 - 80Strong
@581 - 100Very Strong
@6> 100Full Strength

E. Barlines

By the end of a measure, all notes are arranged and closed with a measure marker (|,).

Demo Showcase

I fine-tuned a base LLM on over 80,000 curated music clips using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation). All data were converted into the self-designed music symbolic system, which encodes timing, pitch, duration, and dynamics into compact, language-like tokens. Both fine-tuning and inference use a continuation-based objective to match the autoregressive nature of large language models, enabling stable next-token prediction over long symbolic music sequences.

Introducing MuseStudio

Designed for both professional music producers and music production beginners, MuseStudio is an AI-driven Text-to-MIDI system that redefines how we interact with music production. It integrates Large Language Models, Tool Calling, and the Model Context Protocol to transform the traditional 'click-and-drag' workflow into a natural conversation.

For Professionals

For professional producers, it acts as a creative collaborator—helping turn ideas into full compositions quickly while offering intelligent suggestions to refine structure and expression.

For Beginners

For beginners, it serves as a teacher and guide, making music creation approachable by translating ideas into sound and providing feedback along the way.

Opening up new possibilities for AI-human collaboration in music production, reshaping music-making in the modern era.
